# Animal Voices · Quality & Effectiveness Protocol (v0.9.4)
**Status:** Proposed measurement framework, not measured impact. Last updated 2026-11-08.

## What decision does an evaluation support?
1. Can we safely offer this answer? (Safety/factuality gate)
2. Would a real reader or creator choose to post it? (Blind human preference)
3. Does it clarify the issue for the intended audience without shaming? (Comprehension, trust, civility)
4. Does it contribute to voluntary positive choices and eventually measurable reduced animal-product demand? (Later behavioral trials, not inferred from likes)

## Level 1 · Hard release gates
Any one of the following causes **FAIL**, even if other scores are high:
- A central factual claim is not supported by the linked primary/qualified source.
- A direct quotation is fabricated, out of context, or not verified against the original.
- The reply misattributes a quoted or ironic claim to the commenter.
- It shames a person or presents unsafe individualized medical advice.
- The evidence is insufficient for its confidence level.

A link is **not** proof that the claim is correct. A source card is only editorially curated until a qualified human reviews its supporting passage.

## Level 2 · Blind usefulness scorecard
Two variants of the same answer, shown in randomized left/right order and without version labels. Ask:
**“Which one would you actually post?”** A / B / Neither.

The *optional* six-criterion scorecard applies to the selected/preferred draft:
| Dimension | Weight | What good looks like |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Scientific accuracy & traceable evidence | 25 | Every factual claim supported; uncertainty precise |
| Fit to what was actually said | 20 | Answers question, acknowledges objection, avoids strawman |
| Respect & empathy | 15 | No ridicule of people; values acknowledged |
| Clarity & conversational tone | 15 | Natural German; reader understands immediately |
| Constructive next step | 15 | Genuine question, example, or accessible action when relevant |
| Distinctive voice / memorability | 10 | One warm, original hook, never forced humour |

Score 1–5 per dimension. The weighted score is converted to a 0–100 scale.
**Do not report an overall quality score for a draft until every dimension has a real rating.** Severe factual/safety errors override and set its pass score to 0.

Weights are an *editorial proposal*, not statistically validated. Adjust them only after pilot data and preserve the earlier weights for comparability.

## Level 3 · Prospective field validation
Recruit 10–20 people, including non-vegans and people with different attitudes toward vegan advocacy. Use **100 anonymized real comments** as an initial test set, sampled across genuine questions, nutrition, environment, ethics, cost, misinformation, bait, and unknown topics.

- Two independent reviewers per scientific claim, blind to version.
- Pre-register a definition for **eligible supported cases**; report abstentions, unanswerable and high-risk cases separately.
- Ask each participant to rate a randomly assigned subset; aim for balanced topic and persona coverage; report cases and raters as separate units, never pretend votes are independent when they are clustered.
- For each comment, first check if the agent should respond at all. Then compare drafts.
- Before/after participant comprehension, trust and willingness to continue a respectful dialogue can be measured; a voluntary, consented A/B design is stronger than interpreting raw Instagram likes.
- “I would post this” is the immediate product metric; it is **not** “changed their mind.”
- Report denominators, selection bias, rate of no preference, critical factual errors, inter-reviewer disagreement, and uncertainty intervals for real samples; don't generalize from a small convenience sample.

### Proposed acceptance goals (not attained)
- Critical fabricated citations in reviewed release candidates: **0**
- Unapproved automatic publications: **0**
- Majority preference for the new version across eligible cases (excluding neither), with the proportion choosing neither also reported.
- Real-world publish-worthy rate (A or B chosen) **at least 70%**, as a *target* not a proven threshold.
- At least 80% of invited participants can complete a blind comparison on mobile without instruction.
- Every evidence card has a dated, traceable human review before public scientific claims are described as verified.

## Pilot operational metrics
- Median seconds from pasted comment to usable draft (supported cases only).
- Median human research and approval time, including source verification.
- Number of genuine, civil follow-up questions per 100 *verified published* replies.
- Percentage flagged as irrelevant, shaming, unsupported, or misclassified by participants.
- Volunteer-reported follow-through (checking source, trying recipe, changing a meal) labeled as self-report; never equated to causal behavior or “animals saved”.

## Current limitation
The Response Lab keeps votes **locally per browser** and exports CSV. It is not a shared analytics database; it cannot measure true cohort retention, independent reviewers or Instagram outcomes. Any multi-person trial must obtain **voluntary exports** and aggregate them with an explicitly documented protocol. The lab starts with zero ratings.

## Evidence informing the protocol
- Porter & Wood (2024), *Factual corrections: Concerns and current evidence*; factual corrections often improve accuracy, but downstream behavior change is uncertain. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37988954/
- Social corrections cross-country experiments (2024), miscorrections can reduce belief in true news too. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39242915/
- Altay et al. (2025), *The small effects of short user corrections on misinformation*; average short-comment effects are small and links alone may not increase them. https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/the-small-effects-of-short-user-corrections-on-misinformation-in-brazil-india-and-the-united-kingdom/
- Faunalytics (2026), short digital animal advocacy interventions can move emotions without causing a detectable longer-term behavior change. https://faunalytics.org/the-effects-of-digital-advocacy/

**Decision:** We optimize for truth and kindness first, willingness to use second, and rigorously measured real-world effects later.
